# Skew the slabs, keep the text straight

The sports and stylised games that players call "a proper game" get their energy from a small set of strict rules, and the same rules decide whether the menus stay readable. Each has one angled shape language, one inverted or out-of-palette selected state, very large condensed type with real colour behind it, and a broadcast vocabulary for status. Player sentiment backs this direction: EA Sports FC 27's dark menus were called "corporate" and "gloomy" before launch, College Football 26 was knocked for a "flat, 2D look" and small fonts, and players ask for menus that look like "an ESPN show". The stylised classics show the cost. Persona 5's own director says its menus were "impossible to read at first", Metaphor: ReFantazio's constant motion made some players nauseous, and Guilty Gear Strive's director said the team "simply failed" with a UI that drew 80% of beta feedback. What survives that history is the angle on containers (never on text), motion only when something changes (never looping), text on flat plates measured against the worst pixel behind it, and a selected state that changes several things at once, not only colour. For KANGS this means: the sub-tab row becomes a 56 px band of numbered, 12° slanted slabs, with the current page inverted to a white plate over a gold edge bar. The status text becomes a solid broadcast-style scorebug with a next-opponent chip and a season track. Page headers become full-bleed bands with a 48 px condensed italic title, a giant outlined page word and a Spline or Houdini render. The bands, header and content share one 1600 px grid, and a context rail fills wide screens. Two KANGS-specific findings shape the details. The project already reserves the purple fill for the one way forward, and its purple (`#5b34a0`) reaches only **2.06:1** against the dark page, so purple cannot mark selection and the inversion carries it instead. No source published skew angles, type ratios or motion timings for any named game, so every number below is either measured here or labelled as judgement.

## Players reward colour, couch-size type and broadcast graphics, not novelty

The current sports games give clear sentiment about how menus look, even though no reachable source describes their geometry. **EA Sports FC 27** is the main negative example. When a developer previewed its menus on 31 July 2026, fans asked "Why is the menu so dark?", and Career Mode producer Andreas Wilsdorf replied that EA would "look into the topic" ([Football Gaming Zone](https://footballgamingzone.com/ea-sports-fc/ea-fc-dev-says-theyre-looking-into-games-dull-menus/)). French press called the menus "sombre, lugubre et beaucoup trop institutionnelle" ("dark, gloomy and far too institutional") ([Gamewave](https://gamewave.fr/fc-27/les-menus-de-fc-27-font-deja-polemique-avant-meme-la-sortie-mais-ea-va-se-pencher-sur-le-sujet/)). A forum analysis found the layout "structurally identical" to FC 26, with only "background images and minor color accents" swapped ([EA Forums](https://forums.ea.com/discussions/fc-27-feedback-en/content-feedback-lack-of-innovation--stale-menus-in-fc-27/13825671)). TechRadar's review pleads "Please, EA, add some color to your menus" ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/uk/gaming/ea-sports-fc-27-review)). KANGS's dark theme uses the same recipe that drew those complaints: near-black panels (`#17181c`, `#202227`), grey text, and purple used only on one button. That is the most direct visual risk the evidence points to.

The second signal comes from two games by the same publisher in the same year that got opposite reactions. A player praised **Madden 26** as "clean, modern, and snappy" and said its charts can be read "from my couch without squinting". The same thread faults **College Football 26** for fonts that "are generally too small", a "flat, 2D look" and a feel that is "very sluggish", and asks for a look like "an ESPN show" ([EA Forums](https://forums.ea.com/discussions/college-football-26-discussion-en/consider-using-madden-26s-ui-framework-for-cfb-27/12453412)). What separates them is readability, crispness and speed, not novelty. NBA 2K27 confirms the speed point: its reviewer lists "Navigating menus feels unnecessarily slow" as a con and says nothing about the menus' look ([GamingTrend](https://gamingtrend.com/reviews/nba-2k27-review/)). The most popular piece of 2K menu design in recent years was a return to an old pattern, the right-stick "slide nav" in 2K21 MyNBA, which fans said gave off "serious NBA 2K11 or 2K12 vibes" ([RealSport101](https://realsport101.com/nba-2k/nba-2k21-next-gen-mynba-ui-a-hit-with-fans-new-features-ps5-xbox-series-x)).

Broadcast graphics are the vocabulary players reach for. Reviewers called FC 25's on-pitch overlays "a glimpse of where football broadcasting is heading" ([Push Square](https://www.pushsquare.com/reviews/ps5/ea-sports-fc-25)), and said FC 26's overlays feel "like you're watching a cohesive TV broadcast" ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/sports/ea-sports-fc-26-review)). Players have opened threads asking for "a nicer scorebug" ([EA Forums](https://forums.ea.com/discussions/fc-26-feedback-en/a-nicer-scorebug-ui/13567262)) and for an "official broadcast graphic overlay" on player stats ([EA Forums](https://forums.ea.com/discussions/fc-27-feedback-en/easy-ui-update-broadcast-overlay--player-bio-hub-overhaul-for-fc-27/13695745)). Rocket League's 2023 Play menu redesign moved to "a new squared-off design" of mode cards ([Rocket League](https://rocketleague.com/news/first-look-play-menu-changes-coming-to-rocket-league)). The evidence ends there. No reachable text describes the tab shapes, underline weights, typefaces or currency chips of 2K, FC, Madden, MLB The Show, NHL, Roller Champions or the Mario sports games, and Game UI Database could not be reached. Reconstructions of those games' exact geometry from memory are left out of this report. What the evidence supports is a direction (colour, size, speed, broadcast chips), not a template.

## Stylised menus get their energy from strict rules, then pay to make them readable

Atlus's designers say plainly how much a stylish UI costs. Katsura Hashino says most developers make UI "very simple", while Atlus makes "unique designs… for each and every menu", with "separate programs running for each of them", which "takes a lot of time". He also says Persona 5's menus were "impossible to read at first, so we did lots of tweaking and adjusting so it became legible" ([GamesRadar+](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/jrpg/persona-director-and-jrpg-veteran-says-the-amazing-menus-in-metaphor-refantazio-were-really-annoying-to-make-just-as-persona-5s-were-impossible-to-read-at-first)). The style that carries over is a short list of rules. P5 uses three theme colours (red, black, white) and gives the selected item an accent that sits *outside* that palette, so it pulls the eye. The hovered item "grows larger and gains colour", so focus changes size and colour together. Joker stands behind the pause menu in a pose that changes with the highlighted item ([Mechanics of Magic](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/?p=7280)). Metaphor's lead UI designer Koji Ise described the process at GDC 2025. The team designed the main menu first because it influences everything else, printed every UI sheet on A3 and pinned them in one room to keep the system consistent, and tied each motif to the meaning of its screen: paint splatter for the battle UI's "murderous intent", and the king's face on the results screen because in the story the king is watching ([Game*Spark](https://www.gamespark.jp/article/2025/03/21/150591.html)). For KANGS the motifs should come from basketball rather than generic stripes: court lines, jersey numerals, the scorebug and the whistle.

The legibility history is just as specific. Critics called P5's typography a "readability nightmare", with "dynamic baselines" that put style over information and an extra-bold face that fit localised text poorly. Persona 5 Tactica switched to a Constructivist grid with "a strong, rational sans-serif" and became "more accessible than the original's without sacrificing its creative art direction", though its squeezed tracking and kerning still drew criticism ([Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/gaming/persona-5-tactica-user-interface)). Metaphor shows the limit for motion. A player wrote that its menus made them "physically uncomfortable… almost a bit nauseous", others reported vertigo, and Ise conceded that a reduced mode is "something we understand we'll need to work on and provide in the future" ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/rpg/persona-and-metaphor-refantazios-ui-designer-is-open-to-accessibility-options-for-players-who-find-the-stylish-menus-overstimulating-that-is-something-we-understand-well-need-to-work-on-and-provide-in-the-future)). **Guilty Gear Strive** is the clearest failure. Director Daisuke Ishiwatari said the team "simply failed" with the UI and that **80% of feedback** was about it ([Final Weapon](https://finalweapon.net/2020/02/07/daisuke-ishiwatari-from-arc-system-works-states-that-the-team-failed-with-the-current-ui-of-guilty-gear-strive-currently-working-on-new-ui/)). After launch, its always-animated combo counter was called "an aesthetically pleasing eyesore" that made the player feel they were "missing vital information" ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/guilty-gear-strive-is-too-stylish-for-its-own-good-1847050371)). Its menus also had vague labels ("Player match", "1-player / 2-player") that no styling could fix ([Medium / PatternFly](https://medium.com/patternfly/fighting-games-ux-holds-the-genre-back-a-case-study-into-guilty-gear-strive-s-ux-e0c5e24eae0f)).

Read together, these sources draw a clear line. Angled or skewed text, moving baselines and persistent animation are the documented costs. Skewed containers with upright text, a selected state that changes several things at once, and motion that plays once on a change are not criticised in any source found. The user's four reference images all fall on the safe side of that line once they are translated with care.

## Accessibility numbers the style has to survive

The Xbox Accessibility Guidelines give the measurable floor. XAG 102 requires **4.5:1** for standard text, **3:1** for large text and inactive elements, and **7:1 for every element in a high-contrast mode**. On PC, "large" means **36 px at 1080p**, so every nav label, chip label and sub-line in KANGS counts as standard text. Over a non-solid background, contrast is measured against "the lowest contrasting area", and the fixes are solid backplates or outlines. Images "shouldn't contain text except for Logotypes", and pure decoration is exempt from contrast minimums ([XAG 102](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/102)). XAG 117 asks for "a mechanism to entirely disable" moving or auto-updating content that shares a screen with text ([XAG 117](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/117)). Together these give the design two rules: texture and giant words may appear only where no text sits on top of them, and nothing in the navigation may loop.

Windows forced-colors mode strips more than most stylised CSS expects. It replaces `color`, `background-color`, `border-color` and `outline-color` with system colours, forces `box-shadow` and `text-shadow` to `none`, and removes `background-image` unless it is url-based. It decides colours by native element semantics, so a `<button>` gets `ButtonText` and a `div role="button"` does not ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/forced-colors)). Chrome's own recipe for an animated tab underline draws it with a border "because forced colors mode removes those" other options ([Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/modern-web-guidance/guides/ui-behaviors/anchor-positioning-tab-underline)). A common bug is `border: 0` on custom controls, which leaves them with no outline in high contrast; a transparent border fixes it ([Frontend Masters](https://frontendmasters.com/blog/?p=1120)). KANGS's current selected states use `box-shadow: inset 0 -3px 0` on the top tab and a `border-bottom` on the sub-tab (`src/theme.css`), so the top tab's selected marker disappears in forced colours today. Putting `clip-path` on a focusable element is expected to cut off its focus outline. That is standard painting behaviour but was not confirmed by a fetched source, so the angle belongs on a `::before` plate and the real `<button>` stays a rectangle.

The KANGS tokens were measured for this report (WCAG relative-luminance formula, values from `src/theme.css`). Three results decide the colour plan. First, the inverted plate is very strong: `--ink` against `--page` is **15.84:1** in dark and **15.27:1** in light, and white on black is 21:1 in high contrast. Second, purple cannot mark anything by itself: `--accent #5b34a0` against the dark page is only **2.06:1**, below the 3:1 floor for non-text marks, while white text on it is 8.59:1, which is why the primary button reads as a button. Third, a gold edge works only where it touches the page and not the white plate: `#f2b233` is 9.45:1 on the dark page but 1.68:1 against the white plate, and only 1.62:1 on the light page. A light-theme edge therefore needs a darker gold such as `#8a5a00` (**5.10:1**). Small labels need care too. `--ink-3` passes on the page (5.77:1) but fails on `--panel-3` (**3.87:1**), so scorebug labels on a raised plate must use `--ink-2` (6.26:1).

Semantics stay as they are. A bar that switches screens is navigation, not an ARIA tablist: `<nav>` with buttons or links and `aria-current="page"` is "all you need", and menu roles should be avoided unless the thing is close to a desktop app ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/website-navigation); [MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/docs/Web/Accessibility/ARIA/Attributes/aria-current)). KANGS already marks its sub-tabs with `aria-current="page"` (`src/ui/shell.js` `subnav()`), so the restyle should select on `[aria-current]` and leave the DOM alone.

## The 2026 web can do this without a second engine

Every technique this design needs is now in all major browsers. Same-document View Transitions became **Baseline newly available on 14 October 2025**, when Firefox 144 shipped them ([web.dev](https://web.dev/blog/same-document-view-transitions-are-now-baseline-newly-available)). Firefox 147 (13 January 2026) turned on CSS anchor positioning and added view-transition types ([MDN Firefox 147](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/Firefox/Releases/147)). Container style queries reached Baseline in May 2026 ([web.dev](https://web.dev/blog/baseline-digest-may-2026)). One architectural detail picks between them for KANGS. `render()` in `src/ui/shell.js` rebuilds the whole page after every change, so a CSS transition on an anchored indicator never runs, because the element is new each time. A View Transition, by contrast, snapshots the page before and after the rebuild and morphs a named element between the two. Chrome's guidance is to keep the indicator transition to about 0.2 s and to wrap it in a reduced-motion query ([Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/modern-web-guidance/guides/ui-behaviors/anchor-positioning-tab-underline)). The same rebuild creates a trap. Any CSS entrance animation on the header would replay on *every* click on the page, not only when the page opens, unless it is gated on a change of view.

Typography is mostly a packaging fix. KANGS's upright Barlow Condensed weights 500–900 ship as five TrueType files, `public/fonts/font-0.ttf` to `font-4.ttf`, totalling **430,988 bytes** (measured in the repo). Google's latin woff2 for four Barlow Condensed weights comes to **88,380 bytes**, about 22 KB per weight ([Google Fonts CSS2 API](https://developers.google.com/fonts/docs/css2), measured in the notes). Converting the files to woff2 latin subsets therefore saves roughly 330 KB while keeping the same face. The 800 and 900 *italic* condensed weights are already self-hosted as woff2 (about 23 KB each), so the sports-italic display style costs nothing extra. A new display family is not needed. If one were ever wanted for numerals, Teko is the cheapest at 15 KB variable and the only candidate with Devanagari for the `hi` locale.

Texture and art have cheap, themeable forms. Gradients drawn from tokens re-theme automatically and vanish in forced colours. A greyscale tile used as a `mask-image` over a token-coloured background lets one file serve dark, light and high contrast ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/forced-colors); [web.dev game menu](https://web.dev/patterns/components/game-menu)). Spline's own documentation tells authors to "treat 3D scenes like images or videos", to use at most 1–3 embeds per page and to avoid full-screen embeds ([Spline docs](https://docs.spline.design/doc/how-to-optimize-your-scene/doczPMIye7Ko)). KANGS already runs Three.js for the gym, so Spline and Houdini work belongs in exported stills, not a second WebGL runtime. Blur is the effect to avoid: its cost grows with area and radius, and a blurred layer re-blurs every frame when anything behind it moves ([Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/animated-blur)). Behind the KANGS nav, that something is the live gym.

## Design direction for KANGS

The direction follows Ise's order: build one master first, then spread it ([Game*Spark](https://www.gamespark.jp/article/2025/03/21/150591.html)). The master is the sub-tab band, the scorebug and the Prep page header together, checked with `npm run check:ui -- --shots` in dark, light, high contrast and at 390 px before any other page changes. The earlier usability research sets four constraints that the restyle must not break. There is one purple way forward per screen. Nothing sits deeper than one click from its top tab: no hover menus or drop-downs, and no Persona-style stacked menu that hides the sub-pages. Numbers stay readable at a glance. No store prompt appears outside the Shop. Two smaller rules from the same work also apply: no transition on the path to play, and decorative headers must never push the way forward off a phone screen. In the tables, **E** marks a recommendation that rests on cited or measured evidence and **J** marks one that rests on judgement.

### Top navigation: four slanted group slabs that join the band below

The top bar grows from 56 to **64 px**. The four group buttons (This week, Team, Season, Shop) become slanted plates **44 px** tall, with **22 px** inline padding, a 20 px icon, and labels in Barlow Condensed **800 italic, 18 px, uppercase, +0.03em tracking** (J). Every slant in the interface uses one angle, **12°**, set in `em` as `calc(height × 0.2126)` so that it stays constant under text scaling. Mixed angles look accidental (J). The selected group plate takes the sub-tab band's colour and runs down through the topbar's bottom border, so the open group visibly flows into its own sub-tabs, like a folder tab. A 3 px gold edge sits on its top (J). This marks parent and child by shape, so the two rows never compete with two inverted plates. The empty stretch between Shop and the coach becomes texture: stripes parallel to the slab edges at 6–8% alpha, fading out with a mask, with no text over them (J; texture placement follows E, [XAG 102](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/102)). "Coach Taylor" becomes a profile chip, a 36 px slanted `--panel-2` plate with a whistle glyph and the name at 14 px. It does not show currency, because tokens appear once, in the scorebug ("say a thing once", J). On a phone the groups stay in the existing bottom tab bar. The selected group there gets the same inverted segment instead of the current soft fill and inset shadow.

### Sub-tab band: numbered slabs, an inverted current page and no empty middle

This is the bar the user named. It grows from 44 to **56 px** and gets a solid `--band` fill (`--panel`), so it reads as a broadcast strip rather than a line of text on the page colour. Its inner content aligns to the same 1600 px grid as the page, which on a 1920 px screen removes 376 px of the measured 954–1260 px gap (E, measured in `2026-11-26-blank-space.md`). Each sub-tab is a 40 px slanted plate with a numeral before the label: "01 OVERVIEW · 02 PREP · 03 GAME DAY". The labels are Barlow Condensed **700, 16 px, uppercase, +0.04em**. The numerals are 12 px, weight 800, tabular, in `--ink-3`, which measures 5.77:1 dark and 4.72:1 light (E for the contrast, J for the numerals). The numerals appear only where the order is real: the week's three steps, and the Shop, which the breaks walk through in order. On Season, whose pages have no order, the numerals are dropped (J). The current page inverts: an `--ink` plate with `--page` text (15.84:1), 4 px taller than its neighbours as a size bump, with a **4 px gold edge bar 2 px below the plate**, where it touches the band and not the white (E for every contrast pair; the inversion device follows the user's fighting-game reference). The break name ("Midseason break") becomes a level gold-outlined stamp before the tabs. It is not rotated, because rotated text is the documented P5 cost (E). The way forward at the band's right end becomes a **chevron-ended** purple slab, 40 px tall, pointing right. It differs from the tabs in shape, position and colour, so the one purple button cannot be mistaken for a selected tab (J, under the project's purple rule).

```css
/* theme.css tokens: one angle, an edge colour per theme (contrast measured on --page) */
:root { --band: var(--panel); --tab-edge: #f2b233; }               /* 9.45:1 on #17181c */
html.theme-light:not(.a11y-high-contrast) { --tab-edge: #8a5a00; }  /* 5.10:1 on #eceef2 */
html.a11y-high-contrast { --tab-edge: #ffe28a; }                     /* 16.5:1 on #000 */

.sub-item {                        /* the real <button aria-current> stays a rectangle: focus ring and hit area intact */
  --h: 2.5em; position: relative; isolation: isolate; min-height: var(--h); padding: 0 1.1em;
  border: 2px solid transparent;   /* becomes a visible border in forced colours */
  font: 700 calc(16px * var(--ts, 1) * var(--ls, 1)) / 1 var(--display);
  text-transform: uppercase; letter-spacing: .04em; color: var(--ink-2);
}
.sub-item::before {                /* the slanted plate is decoration only */
  --s: calc(var(--h) * .2126);     /* tan(12deg) */
  content: ''; position: absolute; inset: 0; z-index: -1;
  clip-path: polygon(var(--s) 0, 100% 0, calc(100% - var(--s)) 100%, 0 100%);
}
.sub-item:hover::before { background: var(--panel-2); }
.sub-item[aria-current='page'] { color: var(--page); }
.sub-item[aria-current='page']::before { background: var(--ink); inset: -2px 0; }
.sub-item[aria-current='page']::after {
  content: ''; position: absolute; left: .5em; right: 0; bottom: -8px; height: 4px; background: var(--tab-edge);
}
@media (forced-colors: active) {
  .sub-item::before, .sub-item::after { display: none; }
  .sub-item[aria-current='page'] { forced-color-adjust: none; background: Highlight; color: HighlightText; }
}
```

### Status strip as a scorebug: one solid plate, real numbers, no Shop link

The status text becomes a single scorebug plate on `--panel-3`, with slanted ends and 1 px `--line-2` slanted dividers. It answers the "ESPN show" and "nicer scorebug" requests with the information already shown, plus one new piece (E for the vocabulary, J for the layout). The first segment is a **team block**: the Kangs logo on `--accent-soft` with a 4 px `--accent` edge. This is a tint, never the button fill. Next comes a **matchup chip**, "NEXT · vs REDMOND" with the opponent's crest from `public/images/opponents/`, which fills the band's empty middle with something a coach actually wants to know (J). Then **W–L "1–0"**, **tokens "1,162"** with a coin glyph, and **slots "3"** with three pip shapes beside the digit, so the count is never carried by colour alone (E, [XAG 102](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/102)). Values are Barlow Condensed **800, 22 px, `tabular-nums`, in `--score`** (6.34:1 on `--panel-3`). Labels are **12 px, Barlow 600, uppercase, +0.06em, in `--ink-2`** (6.26:1), never `--ink-3`, which fails on this plate at 3.87:1 (E, measured). A 3 px, 17-segment season track runs along the plate's bottom edge, with played games filled. It shows progress only, not wins and losses in colour, and the text "Game 2 of 17" moves into the plate's `aria-label` and a visible "GM 2/17" value (J). The plate is a `<dl>` of label and value pairs, read once by screen readers (J). The tokens chip is not interactive. Making it a door to the Shop would put a store prompt in the navigation path, which the earlier research ruled out (E, carried over). On a 390 px phone the scorebug drops to record and tokens in a 32 px row under the tabs, as the strip already drops the slots today.

### Page headers: a coloured band, a giant word and a render, with text kept clear

Headers stop being "a plain title plus one grey line" (E, blank-space report cause 5). Each page gets a full-bleed band, at least **152 px** on desktop and at most **96 px** on a phone, with its content aligned to the shared grid. The band is `--panel` with a gradient from `--accent-soft` at the left edge to transparent at 60%. This answers FC 27's "add some color" complaint with real purple tint while leaving the purple fill to the button (E for the sentiment, J for the recipe). The **H1** is Barlow Condensed **900 italic, 48 px, uppercase, line-height 0.95** (30 px on a phone), about 3.2 times the 15 px body text (J, within the notes' 3–5× working range). The sub-line is **16 px Barlow in `--ink-2`**, at most 60 characters wide. Behind the right half sits the **ghost word**: the localised page name from `t()`, never baked into an image, at `clamp(96px, 12vw, 220px)`, weight 900 italic, with a transparent fill and a 0.02em `--line-2` stroke, `aria-hidden="true"`. It is decoration and exempt from contrast, provided no readable text overlaps it (E, [XAG 102](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/102)). At 1200 px and wider, a group render (asset list below) sits at the far right in a 480 × 270 slot, masked to fade into the band, and the text column never overlaps it (J). Up to three page-specific **stat chips** may sit at the header's right, for example Prep's energy and opponent rating, at 28 px value and 12 px label, but never a number the scorebug already shows (J). Home and the live game keep the gym, because the blank-space report found no gap there.

Reduced motion needs two additions to what `a11y.css` already does. The existing `.a11y-reduced-motion *` rule turns off element animations and transitions, but View Transition pseudo-elements sit on the root and need their own rule, `::view-transition-group(*), ::view-transition-old(*), ::view-transition-new(*) { animation: none !important }`. The JS wrapper must also skip `document.startViewTransition` when the `a11y-reduced-motion` class or `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` is present (E for the requirement, [web.dev](https://web.dev/patterns/components/game-menu); J for the exact hook). The static end state is the full design: ghost word, edge bars and texture all stay, they just do not move. `.a11y-no-flash` also turns off the value-change flash.

### Forced colours and the project's own high contrast

These are two separate modes, and both need covering. In **forced colours** (Windows high-contrast themes): hide every decorative `::before` plate, `::after` edge, ghost word, render and url-based mask explicitly, since url images survive. Give each tab a `2px solid transparent` border so it shows as a system-colour outline. Draw the selected sub-tab as `Highlight` with `HighlightText`, using `forced-color-adjust: none` on that one element. Give the scorebug a 1 px `CanvasText` border (E, [MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/forced-colors); [Frontend Masters](https://frontendmasters.com/blog/?p=1120)). In **`.a11y-high-contrast`**, which `settings.js` turns on from `prefers-contrast: more`, every element must reach 7:1 (E, XAG 102). The tokens already switch to black, white and `#ffe28a`. The direction adds `--tab-edge: #ffe28a` (16.5:1), 2 px white borders on unselected slabs so their shapes stay visible, and removes stripes, grain, the court motif, ghost words and renders. The slant and the inversion survive (21:1), so the game keeps its shape language without any decoration (J).

## Conclusion

The most useful finding is that "feels like a game" and "stays readable" are not a trade-off for KANGS, because the sources point at the same small number of controls for both. Atlus spent years making P5 legible and Arc System Works rebuilt Strive's UI after its beta. Both repaired the same things: text set on moving or slanted baselines, elements that never stop animating, and labels that look striking but say little. Those are exactly the parts a web game can leave out while keeping the slant, the inversion, the giant numerals and the broadcast plates that give the reference images their energy. KANGS's existing constraints turn out to be the right design brief rather than an obstacle. The rule that purple means only "go" forces a selected state that does not depend on colour. The full-page `render()` forces motion that plays once per change, which is the rule XAG 117 and the Metaphor complaints ask for anyway.

Two pieces of evidence are still missing. Nobody has published the measured angles, type ratios or timings of the games players admire, so the 12°, 56 px, 48 px and 180 ms values here are working defaults to tune with `check:ui` screenshots, not borrowed facts. And `check:ui` probably reads computed colours rather than pixels, so the rule that keeps texture away from text has to be checked by eye. The practical next step follows Ise's method: build the sub-tab band, the scorebug and the Prep header as one master, look at it in all four themes and at 390 px, and only then spread it to the other twelve pages.
