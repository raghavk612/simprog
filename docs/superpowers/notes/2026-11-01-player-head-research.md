# Carve planes, not blobs, into Kangs heads

The Kangs heads look like squished spheres for four separate reasons, and each has its own fix. The cranium has the wrong shape. The blend radii are big enough to erase every plane change. Decimation spreads triangles evenly instead of putting them on the face. The front-projected face paint smears across the sides. Measured anatomy gives a clear target. A tall young man's head is **about 205 mm long, 155 mm wide (breadth/length ≈ 0.77) and about 230 mm from crown to chin**. In front view the outline goes wide parietal dome, then a pinch at the temples, cheekbones at about 145 mm, angled jaw corners at about 105 mm, and a squared chin. Even when you start from an ellipsoid, the face only appears once brow, sockets, cheekbones, jaw, nose and muzzle are added and cut away at small blend radii. Players are 100–250 px tall, so the head is only about **13–35 px** on screen. At that size silhouette, plane breaks and baked value (dark eye band, shadow under the nose and jaw) do nearly all the work, and lips, nostrils and modelled eyeballs are sub-pixel. My recommendation is a hybrid. Keep the SDF as the source of shape and per-player variety. Re-proportion it, shrink most blend radii to 1–8% of head height, and replace "mesh then decimate" with a fixed, UV'd head template shrink-wrapped onto the SDF. That template should come from a CC0 base head (Blender Studio Human Base Meshes or MakeHuman/MPFB), which needs no attribution. A wholesale switch to a static CC0 head is the fallback, not the first move.

## The sphere fails on proportion before it fails on detail

Artists also start from a ball, but only as scaffolding. In the Loomis method the cranium is a ball with elliptical **flat side planes cut off**. The brow line runs through the middle of the ball, and the face hangs below it in three bands: hairline to brow, brow to nose base, nose base to chin ([Fine Art Tutorials: Loomis Method](https://finearttutorials.com/guide/loomis-method/)). Proko describes building the head by "cutting and adding": side plane, socket planes, cheek and jaw. The side plane runs from the temple to roughly one ear-width behind the ear, and the ear sits in its lower-rear quadrant ([Proko: Planes and Forms of the Head](https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/planes-and-forms-of-the-head/)). The cheek is a slightly convex cylinder tapering to the chin, and the jaw is "a box-like form extending downward from the side planes" ([Loomis guide](https://bhaswanthkota.substack.com/p/the-complete-guide-to-loomis-method)).

Measured data says the same thing in numbers. Using the public ANSUR II male dataset (4,082 US Army men), men 1.88 m or taller average **head length 204.6 mm, head breadth 155.5 mm, tragion-to-crown 134.1 mm, bizygomatic (cheekbone) breadth 144.6 mm and sellion-to-chin 127.2 mm** ([ANSUR II, Penn State OPEN Lab](https://www.openlab.psu.edu/ansur2/)). Across all men the breadth/length ratio averages **0.774**, with a 5th–95th percentile range of 0.72–0.84. A sphere is therefore about 25% too wide for its depth. Cheekbones are slightly narrower than the cranium above the ears (ratio 0.924), and the face is a little shorter than it is wide (sellion–menton/bizygomatic 0.861) ([ANSUR II](https://www.openlab.psu.edu/ansur2/)). Farkas-style soft-tissue norms, back-calculated from a published comparison table, add what ANSUR lacks. **Jaw width (bigonial) is about 105 mm** against about 139 mm bizygomatic, a ratio of about 0.76. Forehead width is about 116 mm, which produces the temple pinch. Nose width is 35 mm (North American White men) to 44 mm (African American men), and mouth width is about 55 mm ([JKAMPRS 2019, Table 6](https://jkamprs.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s40902-019-0191-7/tables/6)).

Two findings bear directly on body_shapes.json. First, real male faces do not have equal thirds. The NAW figures (hairline–nasion 67.1, nasion–subnasale 54.8, subnasale–chin 72.6 mm) give fractions of about **0.34 / 0.28 / 0.37**, so the lower face is the longest band and the African American lower face is longer still at 79 mm ([JKAMPRS 2019](https://jkamprs.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s40902-019-0191-7/tables/6)). Equal thirds read as younger or more drawn. A long lower third reads as an adult man. Second, **heads do not scale with stature**. Men averaging 1.91 m are 9% taller than average, but their head length is only 2.6% larger and their face height 3.8% larger ([ANSUR II](https://www.openlab.psu.edu/ansur2/)). If the pipeline scales the head with player height, tall players get oversized, toy-like heads. The head should be a smaller fraction of a centre's body than of a guard's.

Taken together, the cited numbers give a head-local frame for a roughly 1.9 m, 19-year-old player. Put the origin midway between the ear canals, +Y up, +Z toward the face. The Y positions below are derived by chaining the cited measurements: crown from tragion-to-top, sellion and chin from sellion–menton, nose base from nasion–subnasale, and ear placement from the Loomis rule that the ear spans brow line to nose line. One check: the derived hairline (glabella + 57 mm ≈ +92) lands close to Loomis's "halfway between brow and crown" (≈ +85).

Start with glabella at Z ≈ +110 and the back of the skull at Z ≈ −95, then tune against side-view photographs. The gonial angle and the tilt of the face plane also have no retrieved measurements. Loomis calls the profile line from brow to chin "convex", with the sockets inset about 20–30° ([Fine Art Tutorials](https://finearttutorials.com/guide/loomis-method/)).

## Small blend radii and carved sockets turn ellipsoids into a face

SDF character artists use the same primitives as the Kangs pipeline, but more sparingly. In Inigo Quilez's raymarched character, as documented in a port, the head is "two ellipsoids of different sizes and offsets" (cranium and muzzle) joined with a *small* smooth-min. Paired features are mirrored with `abs(p.x)`. Ears are capsule "sticks" blended "with a very tight radius". The mouth is an ellipsoid **subtracted with smooth-max**, with its height offset by |x| to curve the mouth line ([rt_glsl_puppy.glsl port](https://git.rustybever.be/vieter-v/v/src/branch/master/examples/sokol/04_multi_shader_glsl/rt_glsl_puppy.glsl)). The port's attribution link may not point at the original character shader, so its exact provenance is uncertain. The pattern is the useful part: a few big masses, tight blends, and subtraction for concavities. Quilez's live-coded "Painting a Character with Maths" follows the same order: block the basic shape, sculpt the face, then paint face colours and eyes as a separate pass ([video mirror](https://yt.dorper.me/v/yt/8--5LwHRhjk)).

The polynomial smin makes blend radii easy to reason about. With h = max(k − |a − b|, 0)/k and smin = min(a, b) − h²k/4, the result equals a hard min wherever the two distances differ by more than k. Inside that band the surface sinks by at most **k/4** at the seam ([imaginu sdf.rs](https://docs.rs/crate/imaginu/latest/source/src/sdf.rs); [Stranathan, Polynomial Smooth Minimum](https://ianstranathan.github.io/html/Graphics/polynomial-smin)). Any k close to the size of a feature melts that feature into its neighbour, which is exactly the squished-sphere symptom. The k/4 property gives a scale. With head height H ≈ 230 mm, the radii below keep each fillet narrower than the form it joins. They are design values derived from that property, not published constants, and the iquilezles.org articles could not be retrieved to cross-check Quilez's own normalisations.

| Join | k (× H) | k at H = 230 mm |
|---|---|---|
| Neck ↔ trapezius/torso | ≈ 0.30 | ≈ 70 mm |
| Head ↔ neck | 0.15–0.25 | 35–58 mm |
| Cranium ↔ jaw/muzzle mass | 0.12–0.20 | 28–46 mm |
| Brow ridge, cheekbones, chin pad, jaw-angle pads | 0.04–0.08 | 9–18 mm |
| Eye sockets (smax subtract) | 0.05–0.08 | 12–18 mm |
| Nose onto face | 0.03–0.06 | 7–14 mm |
| Ears | 0.02–0.04 | 5–9 mm |
| Lips | 0.02–0.03 | 5–7 mm |
| Mouth line (smax) | 0.01–0.02 | 2–5 mm |

Smooth subtraction uses the standard duality smax(a, b, k) = −smin(−a, −b, k). Carving B out of A is smax(dA, −dB, k). The concavities are what break the sphere reading: sockets under a brow shelf, the trough between muzzle and cheek, the notch under the chin. Unions can only add bulges. Plane changes too subtle for a primitive (brow mass, philtrum, Adam's apple) can be added as Gaussian bumps, `d -= a·exp(−|q|²/s²)`, with a ≤ 0.3 s so the field stays close to a true distance and meshes cleanly. The IQ ellipsoid approximation, d = k0(k0 − 1)/k1 with k0 = |q/r| and k1 = |q/r²|, is "exact enough for blending/meshing". A scaled round cone gives elliptical cross-sections for nose bridges, jaw bars and lips ([imaginu sdf.rs](https://docs.rs/crate/imaginu/latest/source/src/sdf.rs)).

There is one numeric trap to avoid. A generic SDF recipe in the notes suggested cranium radii of (0.40, 0.45, 0.50)·H. At H = 230 mm that gives a 184 mm wide skull, about 30 mm wider than the ANSUR mean. Where the generic recipe and the anthropometry disagree, use the anthropometry. Cranium radii should be about **(78, 100, 102) mm** in X, Y and Z, with a 10–15% taper of X toward the forehead so the widest point stays parietal and the temples pinch in.

## At 13–35 pixels, value and silhouette beat geometric detail

The screen-size arithmetic decides what the triangle budget is for. A 5 mm feature on a 230 mm head covers about 0.3 px at the low end and under 1 px at the high end. Lip ridges, nostrils and eyelid creases are invisible as geometry. A 10–15 mm brow overhang is about 1–2 px of shadow, which is enough to make a dark eye band. The head's outline (jaw width, chin, ears, neck attachment) is always several pixels and always reads. Art-direction analysis of Team Fortress 2 versus Overwatch reaches the same conclusion from the craft side. Readability comes from a strong light–dark value hierarchy and recognisable silhouettes, and small details should be eliminated "or [have] their values closer together". It also recommends consistency, comparing "the angles of every nose to every forehead" across a cast ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/comparing-team-fortress-2-and-overwatch-art-direction)). So the 1,200 head triangles should go to the brow shelf, the cheekbone-to-cheek plane break, the jaw angle and chin, the nose wedge, the ear stubs and the neck. Mild exaggeration (brow overhang +20–30%, jaw and chin +10–20%, a neck thicker than anatomical) helps an athlete read as athletic at that size, though that is a judgment call, not a sourced rule.

Eyes follow from the same arithmetic. An eye is 1–4 px wide, so a modelled eyeball buys almost nothing. Low-poly practitioners routinely skip separate eyeballs and shape the eye area with a few triangles ([Polycount: how do you make eyes](https://polycount.com/discussion/45409/how-do-you-make-eyes)). Quilez's sphere eyeballs with layered iris and pupil materials work in a raymarcher, not a decimated 1,200-triangle mesh ([rt_glsl_puppy.glsl](https://git.rustybever.be/vieter-v/v/src/branch/master/examples/sokol/04_multi_shader_glsl/rt_glsl_puppy.glsl)). The version that reads best is a geometric socket and brow overhang that catches shadow, holding a slightly convex "closed-lid" surface, with a painted dark almond eye on it. Do not paint a full white sclera, which reads as staring at distance. A specular catchlight is wasted below roughly 40 px head height. If close-up replays ever need blinks, use a UV-offset or texture-swap blink, which is far cheaper than eyeball geometry.

On the shading side, GPU Gems 3 names the cues that stop skin looking "hard and dry". They are subsurface scattering, a multi-lobe diffusion profile (a single lobe looks waxy) and a white, physically based specular with **F0 = 0.028** ([GPU Gems 3, Ch. 14](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/GPUGems3/gpugems3_ch14.html)). That F0 corresponds to an index of refraction of about 1.4 in three.js's MeshStandardMaterial or MeshPhysicalMaterial. The chapter also notes that distant characters can use cheaper approximations. three.js ships an experimental `MeshSSSNodeMaterial` with thickness parameters ([three.js docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/MeshSSSNodeMaterial.html)). It is a node-material route, and its WebGL support was not confirmed. A wrap-lighting term injected with `onBeforeCompile`, remapping N·L to (N·L + w)/(1 + w) with a slight red tint near the terminator, is the dependable WebGL equivalent. At this pixel size the bigger win is **baked ambient occlusion**: dark sockets, under the nose, the mouth corners, under the jaw. Bake it into the albedo or the glTF occlusion map, because that is what makes the face read. Normal-map detail mostly aliases away at 13–35 px, so keep the face texture low-frequency, mipmapped and anisotropically filtered. A 512² head atlas compressed as KTX2 is enough at these camera distances.

## A fixed head template fixes decimation and projection together

The current mesh step works against the shape. Uniform meshing followed by generic decimation spends triangles evenly across the scalp and leaves the face short. In a SideFX forum thread on a VDB-to-game-art pipeline, a user called PolyReduce "the weak link" for its "wierdly triangulated" output. Luiz Kruel of SideFX advised turning on **Equalize Edges** and reducing pieces separately before a final merge ([SideFX forum](https://sidefx.com/forum/post/200097/)). If decimation stays, mesh the head sub-volume at **2–3 mm voxels**: thin features like ear thickness and the nose bridge need at least 2–3 voxels across them, and 1 cm voxels round off the plane changes. That is only about 120³ voxels for a 0.3 m head box. Use adaptivity 0. Reduce the head on its own to about 1,200 triangles with Equalize Edges on, and drive an importance or retention attribute from the SDF's feature-ID mask, for example face 1.0 and back of head 0.2. These PolyReduce parameter names could not be checked against current SideFX documentation.

The stronger fix replaces decimation for the head altogether. Author or derive one low-poly head template once, at about 800–1,200 triangles. It should have real edge flow (eye loops, a mouth loop, a nasolabial line, a jawline, an ear stub) plus fixed UVs and skin weights. For every player, project the template's points onto that player's SDF zero-set. Houdini's **Ray SOP in Minimum Distance mode** does exactly this: it moves each point to the nearest point on the target and is documented for shrink-wrapping one surface onto another ([SideFX docs: Ray SOP](https://www.sidefx.com/ja/docs/houdini/nodes/sop/ray.html)). The VEX equivalent is 5–10 iterations of `p -= d(p)·normalize(∇d(p))` using `volumesample` and `volumegradient`, with a light tangential relax between passes. Concave areas (sockets, under the nose, the mouth corners) are where projection goes wrong ([SideFX forum: projection problems](https://www.sidefx.com/ja/forum/topic/89177)). Cap Max Distance, and put template vertices roughly where those concavities are before projecting. The result is one topology for every player: one UV layout, one weight map for the numpy skinning step, and no front projection. The face texture is painted, or generated, in UV space, so the brows and lips stop smearing across the cheeks.

That template can be generated procedurally in numpy as well. The 2D-SDF face technique (mirrored, y-scaled circles for eyes, Bézier distances for brows and mouth, and offset bands `abs(d − outset) < thickness` for lid lines) produces crisp features with adjustable thickness ([D. C. Hooper, Rick SDF tutorial](https://danielchasehooper.com/posts/code-animated-rick)). Feature parameters can then live in body_shapes.json next to the skull parameters. Each SDF primitive can also return a feature ID (skin, brow, lip, eye), the way Quilez assigns materials ([rt_glsl_puppy.glsl](https://git.rustybever.be/vieter-v/v/src/branch/master/examples/sokol/04_multi_shader_glsl/rt_glsl_puppy.glsl)). The ID gets attribute-transferred to the template and drives both the paint masks and, if decimation stays, the importance weights.

## CC0 base heads supply topology now; most face models are off-limits

The template does not have to be hand-built. **Blender Studio's Human Base Meshes** are CC0: 17 closed, clean-quad meshes that include separate heads, jaws and eyeballs, with UVs, in stylised and realistic versions, the realistic ones carrying multires detail for baking ([CG Channel](https://www.cgchannel.com/2023/06/download-blender-studios-free-human-base-meshes)). **MakeHuman/MPFB** core assets (base mesh, targets, skins) are CC0, and exports need no attribution. The GPL covers only the add-on code. Community assets keep their own licences, though ([MPFB FAQ](https://static.makehumancommunity.org/mpfb/faq/use_in_closed_source.html)). The MPFB base body has 13,380 vertices ([MakeHuman base mesh](https://static.makehumancommunity.org/about/concepts/basemesh.html)). Its "proxy741" topology has only 741 vertices for the whole body ([alternative topologies](https://static.makehumancommunity.org/about/concepts/alternative_topologies.html)), so its head is too coarse to serve as the template on its own. Kenney ([kenney.nl/support](https://kenney.nl/support)) and Quaternius ([LicenseOrg summary](https://licenseorg.com/guide/3d-assets/quaternius); Quaternius' own FAQ was not read) are CC0 but blocky, and fit only as style references.

The licence lines here are sharper than "non-commercial student project" suggests. The restricted FLAME and BFM licences cover research use and forbid making the data "available in any form to any third party". A glTF served from a public game site almost certainly counts as that ([FLAME licence](https://flame.is.tue.mpg.de/modellicense.html)). The Lee Perry-Smith scan is legally usable with credit. It is a recognisable real person, though, and a CC licence covers copyright, not likeness, so it should not become a player's face.

Should Kangs drop the SDF and ship a CC0 head instead? Here is the strongest case for doing so. A Blender Studio realistic head, decimated, already has correct planes, UVs and bakeable detail, and MPFB's CC0 targets offer slider-style variety. Realism on day one would beat several days of SDF tuning. Against that, a static swap discards the parametric control body_shapes.json gives the team. It also means bridging a foreign neck ring to the procedural body, and per-player variety then requires morph targets or several exported heads. The hybrid gets most of both. Use the CC0 head's topology and UVs as the wrap template, and optionally its high-res mesh as a visual reference for tuning the SDF. Keep the SDF as the shape source. Fall back to a full swap, aligned at the neck and joined with Ray-snapped neck points or PolyBridge, with weights attribute-transferred, only if the re-proportioned SDF still reads as a blob in turntable tests.

## Prioritised implementation plan

The steps are ordered by visual payoff per hour. Steps 1–3 work entirely inside the current pipeline and should remove most of the sphere look before any new topology arrives.

The variety ranges come from ANSUR II percentiles and Farkas-group means ([ANSUR II](https://www.openlab.psu.edu/ansur2/); [JKAMPRS 2019](https://jkamprs.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s40902-019-0191-7/tables/6)). The masculinity axis follows forensic skull-sexing traits: heavier brow ridge and glabella, squarer chin, more upright ramus, gonial flaring and a larger mastoid ([FutureLearn](https://futurelearn.com/info/courses/forensic-archaeology-and-anthropology/0/steps/67883)). Keep skull-shape and soft-tissue parameters independent rather than bundling them into ethnicity presets. Variation within a group outweighs the differences between group means: the cephalic-index SD is 0.036, while the White–Black mean difference is 0.002 ([ANSUR II](https://www.openlab.psu.edu/ansur2/)). Verify each step with front, side and three-quarter turntable renders at 13, 25 and 35 px head height, checked side by side against reference photographs. A change that doesn't show up at those sizes isn't worth its triangles.

## Conclusion

The "squished sphere" problem comes from parameters and process, not from too few polygons. Correct proportions and small blend radii change the read more than any added geometry could, because at 13–35 px a viewer sees silhouette and value, not features. The fix scales well. Once a fixed, UV'd template is wrapped onto the SDF, body_shapes.json can describe faces with measured anatomical ratios, the numpy skinning step gets one weight map, and the face paint can be generated rather than projected. That turns per-player variety from a modelling task into a data task.

Some things remain unverified. No retrieved source fixes the skull's front-to-back split around the ear, the gonial angle or the face-plane tilt, so those need tuning by eye. Current PolyReduce parameter names, Quilez's exact smin normalisation, ICT FaceKit's LICENSE text and Blender Studio's head triangle counts were not checked directly.
